Work

Fictional case studies, written as design examples.

None of the companies below are real clients. The briefs are invented so visitors can see how a B2B site might be structured and art-directed. No results, reviews, or traffic figures are claimed.

Fictional case study · Advisory

Pelagos Advisory

Brief. A strategy practice that advises mid-size operators needed a site that felt quiet and exact — closer to a well-edited journal than a lead-gen template.

Direction. A restrained type system, long line lengths for argument, and a services index that can be scanned in a minute. The palette stays in ink, paper, and one warm metal accent so charts and PDFs can follow later.

Pages outlined. Home, practice areas, people, insights, and a contact path with a written brief instead of a long form.

Fictional case study · Export brand

Sawah & Loom

Brief. A textile exporter wanted a public face for overseas buyers: origin story, material standards, and a catalog overview that does not pretend to be a store.

Direction. Warm paper tones, serif headlines, and large material close-ups treated as typography, not decoration. Product groups are described in plain language so a first-time buyer can follow them.

Pages outlined. Home, textiles, standards, atelier, and an inquiry form for trade samples.

Fictional case study · Operations

Lintas Daya

Brief. A regional logistics firm needed to explain routes, industries served, and how a new customer starts — without stock photos of highways or invented on-time percentages.

Direction. A utilitarian grid, high-contrast type, and diagrams instead of stock photography. The contact page asks for shipment type and origin so the first reply can be useful.

Pages outlined. Home, capabilities, industries, network, and a start-here brief.
